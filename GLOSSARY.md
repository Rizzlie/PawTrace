# PawTrace

Terms describing a missing animal and information collected during the search.

## Language

**Case**:
The information about one animal disappearance and the associated search.

**Case owner**:
The person to whom a private case belongs. In the first shared-instance release, each case has exactly one owner.

**Animal profile**:
A description of the animal covering identifying features, age, health, behavior, and familiarity with the area.

**Escape location**:
The single place from which the animal escaped in the incident described by the case. It may remain unknown; later sightings are observations.

**Observation**:
A report of seeing an animal at a particular place and time, together with its information source and an assessment of whether it is the animal being searched for.

**Animal identification**:
The assessment that an observed animal is the animal being searched for, is a different animal, or cannot be identified with confidence. Each observation has its own assessment; one place can have observations with different outcomes.

**Excluded identification**:
An assessment that the observed animal is different from the animal being searched for. The sighting remains information about that place and is distinct from rejecting the report itself.

**Map point**:
A named place that collects multiple history entries from different times, such as seeing the animal, putting out food, and subsequently checking whether the food is still there.

**Point role**:
A label describing a place's significance to the case, such as a potential hiding place, feeding location, or escape location. One point can have several predefined roles at once.

**History entry**:
A record concerning a point at a known or unknown time, classified as an animal observation, action, check, or note.

**Action**:
A history entry describing something a person did at a place, such as putting out food or setting up a trail camera.

**Potential hiding place**:
A point role identifying a possible shelter for the animal; checks of the hiding place are recorded in the point's history. Identifying a hiding place does not mean the animal was observed there.

**Planned search area**:
An area designated for searching.

**Searched area**:
An area where a search took place, described by the time of the check and its result.

**Check**:
A single inspection of conditions at a point or a search of an area, described by its time, result, and a note about what was done. The same point or area can have multiple checks; failure to find the animal does not rule out its later presence.
